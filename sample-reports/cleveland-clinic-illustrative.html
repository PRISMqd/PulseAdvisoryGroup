1. Executive decision brief

Question: What workforce-cost exposure could warrant deeper lender diligence?

Using 17,591 registered nurses strictly as an FTE proxy and 12.8% turnover produces 2,251.648 modeled annual departures, 175,628.544 vacancy FTE-days, and a $135,301,528.32 replacement-cost scenario. The decision-relevant issue is not the size of this arithmetic alone; it is whether enterprise headcount, reported turnover, and demonstration assumptions can be reconciled to a denominator-matched acute-care or facility population. Current data readiness is Limited: the declared workforce basis is a proxy, and independent verification is pending regardless of note content.

What matters now

  • A 1 percentage-point lower turnover assumption changes modeled departures by 175.91 and replacement cost by $10,570,431.90, holding other inputs constant.
  • A $10,000 change in the per-departure cost assumption changes the replacement-cost scenario by $22,516,480.00.
  • A 10-day change in vacancy duration changes modeled patient-care days by 11,258.24.
  • Within this model, priority validation attaches to the workforce denominator and turnover rate, then replacement cost, vacancy duration, and patient-volume assumption. This identifies model-sensitive inputs; it is not a recommendation or professional conclusion.

Formula: 87,814.272 modeled patient-care days × 0.029 reported-event rate × $24,826 severity amount = $63,222,236.38.

11. Decision-context implications

  • The scale of the replacement-cost scenario could justify requesting denominator-matched workforce and vacancy evidence; it is not a credit or covenant conclusion.
  • A lender would need to reconcile enterprise headcount and turnover with facility-level FTE, expense, service-line, liquidity, and reporting-period data before comparison.
